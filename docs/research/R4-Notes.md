## Main Topic - Design Theory & User Experience (UX) Engineering

This week's research covers core User Experience (UX) laws, accessibility guardrails, prototyping tiers, and the impact of layout design. Applying these design theories ensures an intuitive interface that respects the user.

### Core Architectural Concepts & Best Practices

* **Ethical Interaction Design vs. Dark Patterns:** I learned how malicious user interfaces leverage coercive patterns like "confirmshaming" (manipulating users to feel guit during opt-outs/cancellations/deactivations etc.). For an educational app, I must respect the audience and maintain transparent, neutral, or even positive interaction states to build long-term user trust rather than trying to force metrics.
* **Psychological Laws of UX:** Designing layouts requires following established human-computer interaction heuristics. Applying laws like Miller's Law (reducing cognitive load by chunking data) and Fitts's Law (optimizing target element click areas) guides how I will position flashcard modules and review buttons for fast and intuitive navigation.
* **Accessible Modal Architecture:** Dialog modals must be designed with accessibility (A11y) in mind. When a modal opens (such as the prompt window to add a custom mnemonic), I need to ensure a proper keyboard focus, ARIA roles, and clear keyboard interactions (like an `Escape` key hook) to accommodate all users.
* **Prototyping Frameworks and MVP Scoping:** I reviewed the evolutionary path of product design—moving from a Minimum Viable Product (MVP) core into low-fidelity shapes and ultimately into functional high-fidelity prototypes. This reinforces the importance of nailing the central layout before building out complex layers.
* **White Space and Typographic Hierarchy:** Utilizing the W3C and WCAG guidelines for white space patterns improves usability for individuals with cognitive impairments and lowers general visual fatigue. Generous layout padding and correct color contrast ratios drastically elevate the interface text legibility during intense lexical study routines.
